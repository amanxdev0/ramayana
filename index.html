॥ श्रीराम ॥

RAMAYANA

The journey of Rama — prince, exile, warrior, king —
told kanda by kanda, the way Valmiki sang it.
Scroll, and walk beside him from birth to the very end.

Kanda I

॥ कौसल्या सुप्रजा राम ॥

Bala Kanda — the book of childhood

  • Ayodhya, on the Sarayu. King Dasharatha, childless, performs the Putrakameshti yajna. The queens partake of the divine payasa — and four sons are born: Rama to Kausalya, Bharata to Kaikeyi, Lakshmana and Shatrughna to Sumitra.
  • At sixteen, the sage Vishvamitra arrives at court and asks for Rama. Father and son leave Ayodhya to guard the sage's sacrifice.
  • Tataka is slain, and for six nights the brothers stand guard over Siddhashrama — not a single demon disturbs the rites.
  • Ahalya is freed from her stone curse by the dust of Rama's feet at Gautama's ashram.
  • Mithila. Before a hushed court, Rama lifts Shiva's mighty bow — and breaks it. Sita's swayamvara is won; the four brothers wed; even Parashurama's wrath is answered. They return to Ayodhya in glory.

Kanda II

॥ पितृवाक्यपरिपालनम् ॥

Ayodhya Kanda — the book of Ayodhya

  • The coronation is announced. All Ayodhya rejoices — Rama will be crowned Yuvaraja at dawn.
  • One night changes everything. Manthara poisons Queen Kaikeyi's heart; Kaikeyi claims two boons Dasharatha once granted her: Bharata shall be crowned, and Rama shall live fourteen years in the forest.
  • Rama accepts with a serene smile — a father's word is dharma itself. Neither Sita nor Lakshmana will stay behind; all three take up bark garments and leave.
  • The farewell. At the Tamasa they send the chariot back; Guha, the Nishada chief, ferries them across the Ganga in the dark.
  • Dasharatha dies of grief, crying Rama's name. Bharata returns from Kekaya, refuses the throne, and places Rama's padukas upon it — ruling from Nandigrama as a regent, not a king.

Kanda III

॥ दण्डकारण्यम् ॥

Aranya Kanda — the book of the forest

  • Thirteen years among the sages of Dandakaranya. Viradha is slain; the sage Agastya gifts Rama divine celestial weapons.
  • Panchavati, on the Godavari. Surpanakha's advances are spurned — and in fury her kin attack: Khara, Dushana, Trishira and fourteen thousand rakshasas fall to Rama alone in a single battle.
  • Ravana plots revenge. The demon Maricha takes the form of a golden deer; enchanted, Sita begs Rama to catch it. As Rama chases it far, the dying Maricha cries in Rama's own voice — "Ha Sita! Ha Lakshmana!"
  • The abduction. Lakshmana is drawn away; Ravana seizes Sita into the Pushpaka Vimana. The vulture-king Jatayu fights the demon king in the sky and falls, mortally wounded — Rama performs his last rites with his own hands.
  • The search begins. Kabandha is slain and freed from his curse; Shabari offers Rama ber she has tasted for sweetness — and he eats them with joy. At Pampa lake, a stranger approaches through the trees: Hanuman.

Kanda IV

॥ सुग्रीवमैत्री ॥

Kishkindha Kanda — the book of the vanaras

  • A friendship sworn before fire. Hanuman brings the exiles to Sugriva, the vanara prince in hiding. Rama and Sugriva clasp hands over sacred flame — each vows to restore the other's beloved.
  • Vali falls to a single arrow loosed from behind the trees; Sugriva is crowned king of Kishkindha.
  • The monsoon months pass on Prasravana hill. Then Tara's counsel stirs the king — and the search begins.
  • Armies of vanaras pour out to the four quarters. Hanuman's party pushes south to the shore of the endless sea — where the ancient vulture Sampati reveals the truth: Sita is in Lanka, across the ocean.

Kanda V

॥ दृष्टा सीता ॥

Sundara Kanda — the beautiful book

  • The leap. Hanuman grows mountain-tall and hurls himself across the sea — past Mainaka's hospitality, past Surasa's test, past Simhika's jaws — and lands in Lanka by night, small as a cat.
  • In the Ashoka Vatika, he finds her. Sita — thin, steadfast, guarded by rakshasis — unbroken after months of captivity.
  • At dawn Ravana himself comes to threaten her. Sita answers with a blade of grass between them: my heart is Rama's alone.
  • The meeting. Hanuman drops from the tree, gives her Rama's ring, and takes her chudamani jewel for Rama. Then — to measure Lanka's strength — he lets himself be captured.
  • Lanka burns. His tail set ablaze, Hanuman breaks free and torches the golden city, then leaps home across the waves with three immortal words: "Drishta Sita!" — Sita has been seen!

Kanda VI

॥ विजयादशमी ॥

Yuddha Kanda — the book of war

  • The march south. Vibhishana, Ravana's righteous brother, surrenders at Rama's feet and is sheltered. The ocean-god himself advises the impossible: build a bridge.
  • In five days, Nala's vanara engineers raise the Rama Setu across the sea — stone upon stone, and the army crosses into Lanka.
  • War engulfs the island. Angada's final embassy fails; the giant Kumbhakarna is awakened and falls; Meghnada (Indrajit) is slain by Lakshmana after his Nikumbhila sacrifice is broken.
  • The final duel. Gods watch from the sky as Rama and Ravana clash — until, guided by Matali's counsel, Rama looses the Brahmastra. Ravana falls on Vijayadashami. Mandodari's lament echoes over Lanka.
  • Fire cannot burn the pure. Sita walks through the flames of the Agni Pariksha and emerges unscathed; Brahma, Shiva and Dasharatha descend from the heavens in blessing.
  • Home. In the Pushpaka Vimana they fly over the Setu; Bharata rushes to meet them at Nandigrama. The fourteen years are complete — Rama is crowned in Ayodhya, and Rama Rajya begins.

Kanda VII

॥ रामो राजमणिः सदा विजयते ॥

Uttara Kanda — the final book

  • Rama Rajya. Rama rules Ayodhya in perfect dharma — an age remembered as the ideal of kingship, where none grieve and dharma walks the earth.
  • The king's hardest dharma. A washerman's taunt sows doubt among the people about Sita's captivity. Bound by a king's duty to his subjects, Rama — heartbroken — sends the pregnant Sita to Valmiki's ashram.
  • In the forest ashram, Lava and Kusha are born — and raised on the very poem of their father's life, taught by Valmiki himself.
  • The Ashvamedha yajna. Two young boys sing the Ramayana before Rama and the entire court — and the king finally understands who they are.
  • The earth receives her daughter. Summoned to return, Sita calls upon Bhudevi, her mother the Earth — the ground opens in light, and Sita descends into it, lost to mortal eyes.
  • The end of the avatara. Lakshmana departs first; then Rama crowns his sons, walks into the Sarayu river, and the earthly journey of Vishnu's seventh descent comes to its close.

॥ धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः ॥

The epic never ends.

For as long as mountains stand and rivers flow,
so long will the Ramayana be told —
in every telling, Rama walks the earth again.
